## 📜 Overview

Augustus is a multiplayer historical strategy game about turning a fragile Roman Republic into an
empire. Govern provinces, balance the needs of four population classes, and build the food supply,
treasury, and political support needed to unite the Roman world.

## ⚔️ Gameplay

### Win condition

Win the game by becoming **Augustus**, fulfill all three sets of milestones so the Senate can
grant you the title:

- **Military hegemony:** Activate *Imperium Maius*, control Rome and eight Great Cities, and have no
  active legion mutinies.
- **Civic consensus:** Keep average Plebeian, Citizen, and Noble happiness above 60 and Senate
  loyalty above 75%.
- **Economic stability:** Maintain a positive food balance, a treasury above 5,000 sestertii, and
  no active famines.

### Provinces

Each province has its own population and potential for food, metal, and stone production. Great
Cities are centers of administration, trade, and civic building; the countryside supplies raw
materials. The planned campaign uses empire-wide edicts and regional building plans to manage
many provinces without issuing the same order in each one.

### Population

Every population class consumes food, but each contributes differently to your empire:

- **Slaves** are the most productive source of raw materials. Each contributes 50% more labor to
  food, metal, and stone production than a Plebeian in the same province. They do not migrate
  freely, and harsh conditions can lead to revolt.
- **Plebeians** work farms, mines, and quarries, pay a base level of tax, and supply recruits for
  standard legions. As free people, they can move toward provinces with room and better living
  conditions.
- **Citizens** pay more tax than Plebeians and can be recruited into higher-tier forces such as
  cavalry. They can also migrate between provinces.
- **Nobles** generate political influence, and their happiness counts toward civic consensus. They
  normally remain in place, but may flee severe unrest or a siege.

Population grows while food remains available. Overcrowding and low happiness encourage migration;
a prolonged food shortage stops growth and eventually causes deaths and unrest. Imperial edicts are
planned to free slaves, grant citizenship, and elevate citizens to the nobility.

### Resources

- **Food** feeds every population class and the army. Provincial production and consumption feed
  into one empire-wide stockpile; running out starts a famine.
- **Metal** comes from provincial mines and is intended for recruiting and equipping military
  forces.
- **Stone** comes from provincial quarries and is intended for buildings and infrastructure.
- **Sestertii** are the treasury's currency. Plebeians and Citizens provide tax income, while
  armies, construction, and political schemes create expenses.

### Statuses

- **Population** shows the total people in your provinces and how it changes each month. The mix
  of classes determines production, taxes, recruitment, and influence.
- **Happiness** tracks the mood of each class. Taxation, drafting, overcrowding, and famine can
  lower it; civic improvements and public games can raise it. The map's summary highlights the
  least happy class, while the planned victory condition checks civic happiness across free classes.
- **Famine** begins when the food stockpile is empty. A short shortage halts growth; continued
  starvation brings population losses and, eventually, uprisings and mutinies.

### Influence

Nobles generate influence each month, representing the political power available for imperial
actions. The local map currently grants one influence per Noble per month. Spy missions and trades
are planned ways to spend it.
