# Map atmosphere and coastal art

- `terrain-landcover-{nw,ne,sw,se}.png` form a 3840 × 2220 regional crop of [Natural Earth I with Shaded Relief](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-raster-data/10m-natural-earth-1/), [public domain](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/). They supply continuous forest, open countryside, dry grassland, desert, and mountain relief. `scripts/build-landcover.py` pins the global source archive by SHA-256, crops longitude −12° to 52° and latitude 22° to 59°, and adjusts the palette to warm terrain colors. Four 1922 × 1112 tiles keep uploads within the renderer's default 2048-pixel texture limit; one-pixel sampling gutters make bilinear filtering seamless. The tiles meet at longitude 20° and latitude 40.5°. The importer extends neighboring land pixels through the source's empty water areas to avoid blank edges where the two coastline datasets differ. The game clips these textures to playable province triangles, keeping the land texture off the sea and non-playable backdrop. Shared geographic UVs keep detail aligned across borders and zoom levels; translucent ownership and hover washes retain the terrain. The source land cover is decorative modern geographic reference, not a reconstruction of ancient agriculture.

Regenerate the land-cover crop, then the runtime assets, with:

```sh
uv run --no-project --with pillow --with numpy --with scipy python scripts/build-landcover.py
just assets
```

- `clouds.png` is a resized Wikimedia thumbnail of GabrielGGD's [Transparent Clouds Map Without North America](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Transparent_Clouds_Map_Without_North_America.png), [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). Its original transparency is retained; only the outer edges were faded for scrolling.
- `waves.png` uses frame 00 of the `waves3` heightmap from zookeeper's [Seamless looping waves heightmaps](https://lpc.opengameart.org/content/seamless-looping-waves-heightmaps), [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). The sourced grayscale image was converted to faint blue highlights and shadows and repeated four times in each direction. The map samples it through two gently warped, differently rotated current layers with different scales and drift speeds. Broad quiet patches vary their opacity; repeat wrapping keeps the continuous geographic field seamless.
- `ripples.png` uses frame 000 of the directional `waves5` heightmap from the same CC0 source. `scripts/build-water-ripples.py` verifies the archive, maps wave heights to transparent highlights and shadows, and repeats the seamless source four times per axis. A third warped current layer adds subdued fine detail at close zoom. Scattered crests appear for a few seconds, fade completely, and return after independent quiet intervals with different positions, lengths, and angles. The Dead Sea shares this water field, clipped to its shoreline.
- `coastal-gradient.png` is generated from the rendered map geometry by `scripts/build-coast-gradient.py`. It fades the shallow-water tint smoothly into the deep sea.
- `dolphin-jump-sheet.png`, `whale-swim-sheet.png`, `seabirds-flight-sheet.png`, `eagle-flight-sheet.png`, `tern-flight-sheet.png`, and `sea-vortex-sheet.png` are transparent, sixteen-frame 4-by-4 sprite sheets made with the built-in ImageGen tool. Dolphin and whale sequences are one-shot surface appearances. The vortex grows from a faint ring, swirls briefly, and fades back to empty water. Bird flights follow short, randomly directed paths with independent wing phases and a fade in and out. Eagles fly alone; gulls and terns appear singly or in same-species groups of two or three, either side by side or in a trailing line.
